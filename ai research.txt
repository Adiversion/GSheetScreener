# 2. Circuit-band rule needs redesign

The rule says:

> 2% and 5% circuit bands are strictly disqualified; only ≥10% or dynamic F&O bands permitted. INSTITUTIONAL_RULESET_SPEC

The reasoning is understandable—lower circuit bands can make exits difficult—but the implementation has a conceptual problem.

A stock's **current circuit band does not necessarily tell you whether your stop can execute during a gap or locked limit-down event**.

A better risk model would distinguish:

```text
Circuit Band
+
Average daily turnover
+
Bid/ask liquidity
+
Historical gap frequency
+
Historical lower-circuit events
+
ATR
+
Position size / ADV
```

In other words, circuit band should be **one execution-risk variable**, not the primary safety mechanism.

# 3. Gate 2 has an important regime problem

Your regime:

```text

Correction:

Defensive:
```

leaves an edge case:


There is no regime defined for equality.


```text

        ↓
```

That is a **portfolio-level risk policy**, not an objective statement about individual-stock momentum.

I would keep the regime governor, but consider separating:

```text
Market Regime
        ↓
Maximum Portfolio Exposure
```

from:

```text
Stock Quality
        ↓
Whether individual setup qualifies
```

That gives you more granular control.

# 5. Your "200 SMA slope" is not actually a slope

You define:

```text
SMA200,t > SMA200,t-22
```

and call this:

> 200 SMA slope

Mathematically, that's a **directional change**, not a slope.

A proper slope would be something like:

```text
Slope200 =
(SMA200[t] - SMA200[t-N]) / N
```

or preferably a normalized version:

```text
Slope200Pct =
((SMA200[t] / SMA200[t-22]) - 1) × 100
```

Why does this matter?

Consider:

```text
SMA200 today      = ₹100
SMA200 22d ago    = ₹99.99
```

Your rule passes.

But:

```text
SMA200 today      = ₹100
SMA200 22d ago    = ₹90
```

also passes.

Those are completely different trend environments.

### Better

Use:

```text
SMA200[t] > SMA200[t-22]
AND
Slope200Pct >= minimum threshold
```

The threshold itself should be backtested rather than guessed.

# 9. FIP implementation has a serious mathematical problem

This is probably the most important quantitative issue.

You define:

```text
Max 1D Jump Share =
max(ΔPrice1D) / (Price_t - Price_t-40) × 100
```

INSTITUTIONAL_RULESET_SPEC

The denominator is **net 40-day price change**.

That can be problematic.

Example:

```text
Price 40 days ago = 100
Price today       = 110
```

Net gain = 10.

Suppose one day the stock jumped from 100 → 108.

Then:

```text
Max jump = 8
Jump share = 8 / 10 = 80%
```

You reject it.

But if the stock had:

```text
100 → 108
108 → 105
105 → 110
```

the 8% jump represents a large part of the net move even though the overall path could be perfectly legitimate.

Worse, if the 40-day net return approaches zero:

```text
Price 40d = 100
Price now = 101
```

the denominator becomes tiny and the metric becomes unstable.

And if the 40-day return is negative, the metric can become negative.

### This needs redesign.

A more robust measure would compare the largest daily move against **total absolute movement**, e.g.:

```text
MaxJumpShare =
max(|daily return|)
/
Σ |daily return|
```

or use a dedicated return-discreteness statistic.

That would be much more stable.
