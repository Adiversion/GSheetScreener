## LAYER 2 (EXISTING — REPLACED): Dynamic Volatility-Scaled Regime Governor

**Old rule:** Binary SMA₂₀₀ cash switch.
**New rule:** Continuous 3-state regime with **volatility-scaled position sizing**, not just stop-width changes.

| Regime | Condition | Position Scale | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** | Close > SMA₅₀ AND Close > SMA₂₀₀ | 100% of computed size | Standard -5% to -7% |
| **Correction Watch** | SMA₂₀₀ < Close ≤ SMA₅₀ | **50% of computed size** | Tightened to -5% max |
| **Defensive Cash** | Close ≤ SMA₂₀₀ | **0% — strict cash** | Existing stops → -4% |

**Critical addition — Inverse Volatility Scalar within Bull/Correction:**
$$\text{Vol Scale} = \min\left(1.0,\ \frac{\sigma_{\text{target}}}{\sigma_{\text{realized, 20d Nifty500}}}\right)$$

This means even in a Bull regime, if Nifty 500 realized volatility spikes (e.g., budget week, geopolitical shock), position sizes automatically shrink *before* the SMA₂₀₀ breach occurs.

**Why:** Daniel and Moskowitz's study found they could improve upon Barroso and Santa-Clara's constant volatility strategy, building a dynamic momentum strategy that scaled exposure based on forecasts of momentum's mean and variance. They found: "An implementable dynamic momentum strategy based on forecasts of momentum's mean and variance approximately doubles the alpha and Sharpe ratio of a static momentum strategy." Additionally, volatility scaling roughly halves crash severity in backtests and is standard practice in serious implementations.

## LAYER 8 (NEW): NSE Delivery Percentage — The India-Specific Institutional Fingerprint

This is the single most powerful addition. The ratio between delivery and intraday transactions, known as the delivery percentage, is one of the least discussed yet most powerful indicators of institutional intent available in Indian equity markets. Because institutional participants — mutual funds, insurance companies, and long-only FIIs — are structurally unable to trade intraday (their mandates require delivery-based transactions), a high delivery percentage on a given stock on a given day is a near-direct proxy for institutional activity.

### Rules:

**A. Breakout-Day Delivery Spike (Relative, not absolute):**
$$\text{Delivery Ratio} = \frac{\text{Delivery \%}_{\text{today}}}{\text{Avg Delivery \%}_{20\text{d}}}$$

| Delivery Ratio | Classification | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ≥ 1.50× | **INSTITUTIONAL CONVICTION** | Strong pass — prime candidate |
| 1.25× – 1.50× | **CONFIRMED ACCUMULATION** | Pass |
| 1.00× – 1.25× | **NEUTRAL** | Caution flag — needs other confirmations |
| < 1.00× | **SPECULATIVE CHURN** | **Disqualified** — breakout was intraday noise |

**Why relative, not absolute:** This screen specifically isolates stocks where delivery percentage has spiked significantly above their own 10 or 20-day average delivery percentage — not just high in absolute terms, but anomalously high relative to that stock's historical norm.

**B. Multi-Day Silent Accumulation Pattern (most powerful signal):**
The most powerful delivery percentage signal is not a single-day spike — it is three to five consecutive sessions of above-average delivery in a stock trading in a tight price range with declining total volume.

$$\text{Silent Accumulation} = \begin{cases} \text{TRUE} & \text{if } \text{Delivery\%}_{t-i} > \text{Avg Delivery\%}_{20} \text{ for } i = 0..2 \\ & \text{AND price range}_{3\text{d}} \le 1.5 \times \text{ATR}_{14} \\ & \text{AND Volume}_{t} < \text{Volume}_{t-2} \end{cases}$$

Stocks passing this multi-day pattern get a **Tier 1 (Prime)** classification regardless of single-day delivery ratio.

**Data source:** NSE bhavcopy, published free daily by ~6:00–7:00 PM IST. NSE and BSE publish daily delivery data for all equity scrips after market hours, typically by 6:00–7:00 PM IST.

## LAYER 9 (NEW): Wyckoff Volume Spread Analysis — Effort vs. Result Trap Detection

This layer catches the single most common failure mode in mechanical breakout systems: the **upthrust** (false breakout on volume that closes weak).

### The Core Principle:
In a healthy trend, effort and result are in harmony. High volume should produce a wide price spread in the direction of the trend. This confirms the trend's strength. But when effort and result are in conflict, it's a red flag. This divergence is often the earliest sign of a potential change in trend.

### Codifiable Rules (Daily bars):

**A. Breakout Bar Quality Score:**

| Check | Pass | Fail (Upthrust Signature) |
|---|---|---|
| Close position within bar range | Close in **top 33%** of daily range | Close in **bottom 50%** of daily range |
| Volume vs. 20d average | ≥ 1.0× | Volume ≥ 1.5× BUT close in bottom half = **effort without result** |
| Spread (High-Low) vs ATR₁₄ | ≥ 0.8× ATR (wide spread = genuine move) | < 0.5× ATR on high volume = **absorption / distribution** |

Absorption bars (high volume, narrow spread) reveal a battle between buyers and sellers — the side absorbing the effort usually wins.

**B. VSA Event Classification:**

| Pattern | Detection | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Demand Bar (SOS)** | Wide spread up + high volume + close near high | Wide spread up bar on high volume with close near the high. Indicates strong buying pressure and institutional demand entering the market. **→ PASS** |
| **No Demand** | Narrow spread up + low volume | Narrow spread up bar on low volume. The market attempted to rise but lacked buying conviction. Bearish implication, especially in a downtrend context. **→ DISQUALIFIED** |
| **Upthrust / Supply** | Wide spread down + high volume + close near low | Wide spread down bar on high volume with close near the low. Indicates strong selling pressure and institutional supply being released. **→ DISQUALIFIED** |
| **Absorption** | High volume + narrow spread | Institutional accumulation if at support, distribution if at resistance — **flag for manual review** |

**C. T+1/T+2 Retest Confirmation (Post-Breakout Hold):**

This is the most important single rule for eliminating traps:

$$\text{Retest Pass} = \text{Close}_{T+1} > \text{Breakout Pivot Level} \text{ AND } \text{Close}_{T+2} > \text{Breakout Pivot Level}$$

If price wicks above the pivot on Day 0 but closes back inside the base on T+1 or T+2 → **UPTHRUST CONFIRMED → DISQUALIFIED**.

An "upthrust" is the distribution equivalent of the spring — a final fake breakout above the ceiling that's immediately reversed to trap late longs.

### Rules:
